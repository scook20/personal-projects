Voice Agent for Field Service Close-Out

Commercial services contractor — via partner engagement

When a technician finishes a job, the billing team has to chase them by phone for the handful of details an invoice needs. This calls the tech instead. The agent asks a fixed set of questions, and the answers come back mid-call as structured data, land in the job record in the company's ERP, and flag the ones that need a human.

The hard part was writing into a system that already holds real people's work. The ERP only accepts a whole-note overwrite, and roughly a third of those notes already had a biller's own writing in them. So it reads the note first, prepends to it, and refuses to write at all if that read fails — an automation that can silently destroy someone's work is worse than no automation.

Next on the roadmap: the close-out call is the first piece of a larger program. The anchor build is an invoice agent — it reads the finished job, works out what the customer's contract covers and what is billable, and drafts the invoice on hold so a person still releases every one. The model classifies and names the rule it applied; a deterministic step does all the money math, so no dollar figure is ever produced by a language model. After that comes the dispatch side: turning inbound service email into scheduled appointments, and after-hours coverage that knows who is on call.

quill — Meeting Notes That Never Leave My Mac

Personal build — Engineer

A Mac app that records my meetings and writes up the notes. It records my microphone and the other person's audio as two separate tracks, which is a simple trick that tells you who said what without any AI guessing at voices. The transcription runs on the laptop itself — about 20 seconds of work per hour of audio — so no recording is ever uploaded anywhere.

I picked the parts by testing, not guessing. Apple's built-in on-device model went first: on a real 12,000-word meeting it credited the wrong person with half the conversation and dropped the details, so note-writing goes to Claude and the local model stays as the offline backup. That is the one thing that leaves the machine, it is text only, and it can only reach a single approved address.
